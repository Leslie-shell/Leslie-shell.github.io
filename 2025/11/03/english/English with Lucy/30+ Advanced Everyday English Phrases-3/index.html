Advanced Everyday English Phrases-3

  • C1-C2高级形容词精讲

    Ambivalent

    • 音标：/æmˈbɪv.əl.ənt/
    • 含义：矛盾的；摇摆不定的（having or showing mixed feelings or contradictory ideas）
    • 例句：
      • I feel ambivalent about moving abroad—excited but also nervous.
      • She has ambivalent feelings towards her hometown.

    Capricious

    • 音标：/kəˈprɪʃ.əs/
    • 含义：善变的；反复无常的（given to sudden and unaccountable changes of mood）
    • 例句：
      • The capricious weather made planning the picnic impossible.
      • His capricious nature made him difficult to work with.

    Derogatory

    • 音标：/dɪˈrɒɡ.ə.tər.i/
    • 含义：贬低的；不敬的（showing a critical or disrespectful attitude）
    • 例句：
      • He made several derogatory remarks about her appearance.
      • The article contained derogatory comments about immigrants.

    Esoteric

    • 音标：/ˌes.əˈter.ɪk/
    • 含义：深奥的；难懂的（intended for or understood by only a small number of people）
    • 例句：
      • The professor’s esoteric theories confused most students.
      • This ancient text contains esoteric knowledge.

    Flippant

    • 音标：/ˈflɪp.ənt/
    • 含义：轻率的；无礼的（not showing a serious or respectful attitude）
    • 例句：
      • His flippant response angered his teacher.
      • She regretted her flippant comment about the serious situation.

    Grandiose

    • 音标：/ˈɡræn.di.əʊs/
    • 含义：浮夸的；不切实际的（impressive but impractical and lacking substance）
    • 例句：
      • The dictator had grandiose plans for rebuilding the city.
      • His grandiose promises never materialized.

    Histrionic

    • 音标：/ˌhɪs.triˈɒn.ɪk/
    • 含义：戏剧化的；做作的（overly theatrical or melodramatic）
    • 例句：
      • Her histrionic reaction to the minor accident was unnecessary.
      • He gave a histrionic performance that lacked sincerity.

    Incongruous

    • 音标：/ɪnˈkɒŋ.ɡru.əs/
    • 含义：不协调的；不一致的（strange and not suitable in a particular situation）
    • 例句：
      • The modern furniture looked incongruous in the historic castle.
      • His casual attire was incongruous with the formal occasion.

    Juxtaposed

    • 音标：/ˈdʒʌk.stə.pəʊzd/
    • 含义：并置的；对比的（placed together for contrast and comparison）
    • 例句：
      • The exhibition featured juxtaposed images of wealth and poverty.
      • The artist juxtaposed bright and dark colors in her painting.

    Laudable

    • 音标：/ˈlɔː.də.bəl/
    • 含义：值得赞扬的（deserving praise and commendation）
    • 例句：
      • Her efforts to help the homeless were laudable.
      • The company’s environmental initiatives are laudable.

    Mercurial

    • 音标：/mɜːˈkjʊə.ri.əl/
    • 含义：多变的；无常的（often changing in an unexpected way）
    • 例句：
      • The CEO’s mercurial temperament made employees nervous.
      • British weather is famously mercurial.

    Nonchalant

    • 音标：/ˈnɒn.ʃəl.ənt/
    • 含义：冷静的；漠不关心的（behaving in a calm, relaxed way without worry）
    • 例句：
      • He tried to appear nonchalant about the exam results.
      • Her nonchalant attitude surprised everyone in the crisis.

    Pernicious

    • 音标：/pəˈnɪʃ.əs/
    • 含义：有害的；恶性的（having a harmful effect, especially gradually）
    • 例句：
      • The pernicious effects of smoking are well documented.
      • Racism is a pernicious problem in our society.

    Quixotic

    • 音标：/kwɪkˈsɒt.ɪk/
    • 含义：不切实际的；空想的（exceedingly idealistic and unrealistic）
    • 例句：
      • His quixotic quest to end world poverty continued for years.
      • The plan was dismissed as quixotic and impractical.

    Resplendent

    • 音标：/rɪˈsplen.dənt/
    • 含义：灿烂的；华丽的（attractive and impressive through being richly colorful）
    • 例句：
      • The queen appeared resplendent in her jeweled crown.
      • The garden was resplendent with autumn colors.

    Subservient

    • 音标：/səbˈsɜː.vi.ənt/
    • 含义：卑躬屈膝的；顺从的（prepared to obey others without question）
    • 例句：
      • She refused to be subservient to her husband.
      • His subservient behavior towards his boss was uncomfortable to watch.

    Supercilious

    • 音标：/ˌsuː.pəˈsɪl.i.əs/
    • 含义：傲慢的；目中无人的（behaving as though superior to others）
    • 例句：
      • The waiter gave us a supercilious look when we asked for tap water.
      • Her supercilious attitude made her unpopular with colleagues.

    Tenacious

    • 音标：/təˈneɪ.ʃəs/
    • 含义：坚韧的；固执的（keeping a firm hold; not giving up easily）
    • 例句：
      • Her tenacious pursuit of justice finally brought results.
      • The old tree had tenacious roots that survived the storm.

    Ubiquitous

    • 音标：/juːˈbɪk.wɪ.təs/
    • 含义：无处不在的（very common or found everywhere）
    • 例句：
      • Mobile phones have become ubiquitous in modern life.
      • The company’s logo was ubiquitous throughout the city.

    Vicarious

    • 音标：/vɪˈkeə.ri.əs/
    • 含义：替代的；感同身受的（experienced through the feelings of another person）
    • 例句：
      • She lived vicariously through her daughter’s adventures.
      • Reading travel books gave him vicarious pleasure.
